Session 1

Datasheets

  • List what a datasheet of a de-identified release must answer

01
What a recipient cannot see

A file without its documentation invites the wrong use

  • Waad receives 59,707 rows: she cannot see the 293 suppressed records
  • She cannot see that income is as recorded, overstated for the self-employed
  • She cannot see that the officer decision is historical, not a reviewed outcome

The datasheet answers

Why the data exist, what is in them, how they were made and treated, what risk remains, and what they must not be used for.

Gebru and colleagues, "Datasheets for Datasets" (2021), propose the questions; we use their sections plus two that a de-identified release needs. Ask: which of the three hidden facts would change Waad's conclusions most? The suppression falls on non-Saudi applicants in small regions, so a regional analysis of nationality is biased by the treatment itself.

The sections

Seven sections, two of them for de-identified data

Motivation

Who asked, for what purpose, who approved.

Composition

Records, fields released and removed.

Collection

The source and what its fields really record.

De-identification

Risk before, treatment, risk after, utility.

Residual risk

What the treatment does not cover.

Uses · Maintenance

Two sections: allowed and forbidden uses; contact and code.

Composition, Collection, Uses and Maintenance follow Gebru and colleagues; De-identification and Residual risk are this course's additions, tied to the national anonymisation guideline, which treats pseudonymised data as personal data. Lab 4.1 checks that each section is present and not empty.

One number is not a guarantee

"k = 5" means little without what it was measured on

  • The quasi-identifiers: birth band, gender, nationality, region
  • The suppression: 293 records, and who they were
  • What k does not cover: a class that shares one income or one disability value

Residual risks in the log

Attribute disclosure in classes with one value; outside files holding other fields; suppression falling on non-Saudi applicants in small regions.

What goes wrong. A datasheet that says "anonymised" and stops there. The extract is still personal data and stays Restricted.

The Data Classification Policy puts personal data under Restricted; the extract was Restricted B before treatment, and treatment lowers the risk, not the class. The guideline's own examples of de-identification techniques (generalisation, aggregation, masking) are what Day 2 applied.

A reviewer accepts a limit that is stated with its reason; a limit discovered later reads as concealment. This list is task 5 of Lab 4.1. The stretch adds the distribution terms from the sharing agreement: research use only, no linkage, destruction after 24 months.

Session 2

Model cards

  • Write the intended and out-of-scope uses of a model
  • Report disaggregated results with intervals
  • State the fairness criterion and what it costs
02
Mitchell and colleagues, 2019

A model card tells a reviewer what the model is for and where it fails

  • Model details and intended use
  • Metrics: which, against what outcome
  • Quantitative analyses: results by group, with intervals
  • Fairness: the criterion, the mitigation, its cost
  • Out-of-scope uses and caveats

Ahliya today

No card: only a validation report the audit could not reproduce.

The fairness section

State the criterion, the gap, the mitigation, and what it costs

Held to equal opportunity: equal false negative rates, because the harmful error is declining an eligible applicant. Largest group gap 12.9 points; group thresholds reduce it to 10.0 at the same accuracy, using gender and nationality, which needs approval. The lasting fix is verified income.

The paragraph is generated by Lab 4.2 from the Lab 3.4 reference comparison, so its numbers cannot drift from the audit. It contains the four things a reviewer needs: criterion and reason, size of the problem, the measure and its result, and its cost.

Limits

Out-of-scope uses are the ones someone will try

  • Declining an application without an officer's review
  • Scoring applications for another programme
  • Ranking applicants against each other for a fixed budget

Caveats

Trained on historical decisions. Measurement bias in self-employed income, historical bias on family homes, labelling bias in one office, a feedback loop through auto-declines.

Each caveat names a bias type from the national AI Bias Reference Guide, found on Day 3. The first out-of-scope use is the one in production today: 37.4% of applications were auto-declined below 0.2, finding F5. Ask the room which use a new manager would most likely try.

Session 3

Impact assessment

  • Score a risk's likelihood and impact on the national framework's scales
  • Choose a treatment and score the residual risk
  • Name a human reviewer for every high risk
03
National AI Risk Management Framework

Identify, assess, treat, monitor: and assess again after treatment

1 · Identifythe risk, who is affected, the evidence
→
2 · Assesslikelihood × impact, a level and a band
→
3 · Treatavoid, mitigate, transfer or accept
→
4 · Monitorthe residual risk, its owner, its reviewer

Press once per step. The framework (April 2026) sets the cycle and the scales. Our four risks come from the week: re-identification (Days 1 and 2), wrongful declines (Day 3), the dashboard budget (Day 2), and the synthetic file (Day 2). Identification is done for you in Lab 4.3, so the lab time goes to scoring.

The scales

Two scores from 1 to 4, and their product is the level

ScoreLikelihoodImpact
1RareLow: limited, contained quickly
2UnlikelyMedium: tangible, contained in reasonable time
3LikelyHigh: substantial, needs an organisational response
4Almost certainCatastrophic: severe, wide, possibly irreversible

Bands: 1 to 2 low · 3 to 6 medium · 8 to 12 high · 16 catastrophic.

Short forms of the framework's descriptions. Note that a level of 5, 7, 10, 11 or 13 to 15 cannot occur: products of 1 to 4 skip them. Likelihood is argued from exposure, the controls in place, how easily the harm happens, and precedents; impact from severity, breadth and reversibility.

A score needs a reason

Argue every score from a measured number

R2 · wrongful declines

Likelihood 4: it is happening now, 30% of eligible non-Saudi women declined. Impact 3: support delayed or lost. Level 12, high.

R4 · the synthetic file

Likelihood 2: only a regeneration with another setting copies rows. Impact 3: leaf one copied 92.8%. Level 6, medium.

What goes wrong. Scores by feeling, then adjusted until the table looks balanced. The register check in Lab 4.3 rejects a reason with no number in it.

The evidence lines are in data/risks.json and come from the week's reference runs. Ask a table to defend R2's likelihood at 3, then ask the room why measured harm is "almost certain". The answer is the checkpoint's.

Session 4

Review by someone else

  • Rerun a colleague's result from their notebook and data
  • Find the undocumented choices that change a number
  • Record documentation gaps that a reviewer can act on
04
Reproducibility as evidence

A result counts when someone else can rerun it and get the same number

  • The code and the data, as used
  • A pinned environment: uv.lock in this course
  • Every seed, every threshold, every filter
  • A log of the run that was reported

The capstone

The assessor picks a slice and you recompute one disparity live, from your repository.

Reproducibility is the 10% criterion of the capstone rubric, but it underpins the other 90%: an audit nobody can rerun is an opinion. This course pins its environment with uv.lock and seeds every random step with 309.

From Lab 4.4's variants. The filter drops 633 rows, 65% of them women against 34% overall, and many are eligible applicants Ahliya misses. The README's "Ahliya's threshold" is wrong: the code uses 0.45. A third gap: the bootstrap has no seed, so the interval changes on each run.

Recording a gap

A useful gap says where, by how much, and what to write

Where

load(), the notna() filter.

Effect

Gap -2.9 with the filter, +0.6 without it.

Fix

Keep the rows, or state the filter and who it removes.

What goes wrong. "Results not reproducible." True, and of no use to the author.

Lab 4.4 records four gaps in these columns: the filter, the threshold, the seed, and the missing versions. The check requires at least three, with a number in the ones that move the result. The tone is a colleague's, not an examiner's: the author fixes what the review names.

The corrected result

On all rows at 0.5, no gender gap: the claim does not hold

On all 12,000 reviewed applications at Ahliya's threshold of 0.5, the false negative rate is 0.227 for women and 0.228 for men (gap -0.002, 95% bootstrap interval -0.020 to +0.018, seed 309).

Printed by Lab 4.4. The interval includes zero. The audit's real gap, non-Saudi women, is invisible at this margin in both versions. The capstone review asks for the same: a stated result, the choices it rests on, and a seed.

Checkpoint

Which gap explains the different number?

A peer reports an FNR gap of -2.9 points; your rerun on all rows at 0.5 gives -0.2. The bootstrap is unseeded, the threshold is 0.45, and 633 rows with missing income are dropped.

The dropped rows: removing them alone takes the gap from +0.6 to -2.9
The unseeded bootstrap, because each run differs
The threshold of 0.45, because it differs from the documented 0.5
A different pandas version on the peer's machine
Why. At the peer's threshold, keeping the rows gives +0.6; dropping them gives -2.9, because they are mostly women Ahliya misses.

Answer A. C is the gap participants find first, since it contradicts the README; it matters for documentation but barely moves this number. Measuring each choice separately is what the variants table is for.
