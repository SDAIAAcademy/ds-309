What an audit counts

For a support decision, the two errors do different harm

False negative

An eligible applicant declined by Ahliya. The harm the fund exists to prevent. FNR = missed eligible / all eligible.

False positive

Support passed to someone not eligible. A cost to the fund, caught later by an officer. FPR = wrongly passed / all not eligible.

In the reference data: 12,000 reviewed applications, 6,341 eligible, 5,280 passed by Ahliya at a threshold of 0.5.

The reviewed outcome is the audit's ground truth: in practice a reviewed sample is expensive, which is why audits run on samples. Ask the room which error Bashayer would rather reduce; most will say false negatives. Keep that answer: it decides Session 2's criterion.

What "fair" can mean

Three criteria, each reasonable, each equalising something different

CriterionEqual across groupsPlain words
Demographic parityselection ratepass the same share of each group
Equal opportunityfalse negative ratemiss eligible people equally often
Equalised oddsfalse negative and false positive ratesboth errors equal
Predictive parityprecisiona pass is equally reliable for each group

Equal opportunity is the name Hardt, Price and Srebro gave to equal true positive rates, which is the same as equal false negative rates. Sources: Hardt, Price and Srebro, Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning.

How to choose

Choose the criterion from the decision and the harm of each error

  • SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles ask that fairness metrics be chosen at design time from the kind of decision and the harm and benefit of correct and incorrect predictions
  • Ahliya allocates support by need; the harmful error is the missed eligible applicant
  • So: equal opportunity, with the selection gap explained by need

When parity fits

When need is believed equal and the labels are suspect, or when a fixed opportunity is shared out.

The principles also ask for the allowed threshold of the metric to be defined at the plan-and-design stage, before results are seen. Ahliya had none: that becomes a gap in the model card on Day 4. Sources: AI Ethics Principles (SDAIA), fairness principle, plan and design.

Why the type matters

The same gap can have two causes that need opposite fixes

Measurement or labelling?

Measurement: a feature is recorded wrongly (fix the recording). Labelling: the outcome used for training is wrong (fix the labels).

Historical or labelling?

Historical: labels followed a rule that has since changed (retrain on the current rule). Labelling: labels were wrong when made (relabel or review).

These two tests are the core of Lab 3.3, task 3. Ask for an example from participants' own work of a gap they explained without checking which cause it was.

Mitigation

You can act before training, during it, or after it

Before: pre-processing

Change the training data: reweighting, resampling, relabelling, fixing a feature.

During: in-processing

A constraint in the fitting, such as Fairlearn's reductions. Named here, not practised.

After: post-processing

Change the decision rule: a threshold per group.

The lab tries one pre-processing and one post-processing method, as the catalog entry asks. In-processing needs more time than a 25-minute lab allows. All three are in the AI Ethics Principles' list of fairness tools through Fairlearn, AI Fairness 360 and Aequitas. Sources: Fairlearn documentation; AI Ethics Principles, Annexure A.

Pre-processing

Reweighting makes group and label independent in the training data

pg  = df["group"].value_counts(normalize=True)
py  = df["y"].value_counts(normalize=True)
pgy = df.groupby(["group", "y"]).size() / len(df)
w   = pg[g] * py[y] / pgy[(g, y)]
model.fit(X, y, logisticregression__sample_weight=w)
  • Rare combinations, such as approved non-Saudi women, weigh more
  • The weights average 1
  • It can only fix what the labels' balance causes

Kamiran and Calders' method. The last bullet is the lesson of the lab: Session 3 found the gap comes from income features, so reweighting should not help much. Sources: Kamiran and Calders, Data preprocessing techniques for classification without discrimination (2012).

Post-processing

A threshold per group, tuned on one half and reported on the other

  • Target: each group's FNR equal to the overall 22.5%
  • Tuned thresholds: non-Saudi women 0.468, Saudi women 0.511, non-Saudi men 0.470, Saudi men 0.499
  • Reported on reviewed applications not used to tune

A legal question, not only a technical one

The rule now uses gender and nationality at the moment of decision. The data protection officer and legal counsel decide whether that is allowed and how it is explained.

From Lab 3.4. Tuning and reporting on the same rows would overstate the effect. The AI Ethics Principles ask that sensitive attributes be kept out of model inputs, or that their use be justified: a group threshold is such a use, so it needs that justification in writing.
