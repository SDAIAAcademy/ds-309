# Answer key (trainers only)

The planted problems of the synthetic data, which lab finds each one, and the numbers each lab's
solution prints in full mode. A build for trainees leaves this folder out. All numbers come from
the reference runs with seed 309 and the versions pinned in `uv.lock`; quick mode (`YUSR_QUICK=1`)
gives nearby values for Labs 2.2 to 2.4.

## Planted problems: Yusr Housing Support Fund

| Problem in the data | Where | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| Direct identifiers typed into free-text case notes (2,470 notes) | `applicants.csv.gz`, `case_note` | Lab 1.1 |
| Birth date, gender and postcode make almost every applicant unique, and 3,000 applicants appear in a public register | `release_a_research_extract.csv.gz`, `public_register.csv` | Labs 1.3, 1.4 |
| District counts with very small cells (smallest 2) | extract E3 of Lab 1.2 | Labs 1.2, 2.2 |
| Self-employed income recorded at 1.5 to 2 times its verified value | `income_audit.csv` (600 rows) | Lab 3.3, F1 |
| Officers applied a family-home rule until July 2024 (7,773 rows decided under it) | `ahliya_train.csv.gz` | Lab 3.3, F2 |
| The Qassim office declined eligible women at a higher rate (169 flipped decisions) | `ahliya_train.csv.gz`, `ahliya_scored_test.csv` | Labs 3.1, 3.3, F3 |
| Income missing more often for women, most often for non-Saudi women; Ahliya learnt that missing income means decline | all Ahliya files | Labs 3.1, 3.4, 4.4 |
| Validation on officers' decisions in two regions only (reported accuracy 0.881) | `ahliya_validation_report.json` | Lab 3.3, F4; Lab 4.2 |

| A peer's result rests on an undocumented filter and threshold | `peer/` | Lab 4.4 |

## Expected results

**Day 1**

- 1.1: the tag table and the drop decisions (AI-off); 2,470 notes carry an identifier.
- 1.2: applicant table Restricted B; research extract Restricted B; district counts Restricted C,
  because the smallest cell is 2.
- 1.3: 2,976 of 3,042 claimed links correct (precision 0.978, 99.2% of register applicants
  re-identified). On birth year instead of birth date: 1,595 claimed, 506 correct (precision
  0.317, success 0.169).
- 1.4: unique records 59,458 / 2,152 / 7 for releases A, B and C; average risk 0.996 / 0.129 / 0.004.

**Day 2**

- 2.1: five-year birth bands and region; 293 records suppressed; k = 5; largest change in a key
  statistic -1.01% (mean income, Northern Borders); 0 links after treatment.
- 2.2: mean absolute error 9.98 / 1.92 / 0.85 at epsilon 0.1 / 0.5 / 1; relative error in the ten
  smallest districts 16% / 3.1% / 1.5%; recommended 0.5.
- 2.3: split by need gives Q3 0.857 of the budget; worst relative error 0.61%. Spreading the same
  yearly budget over twelve monthly releases gives a worst error of about 7.2% per release.
- 2.4: leaves of one row copy 92.8% of training rows, membership AUC 0.819 (do not release);
  leaves of 200 copy 0.10% (holdout 0.05%), membership AUC 0.511, a model trained on the synthetic
  file reaches AUC 0.933 against 0.936 on the real one (release).

**Day 3**

- 3.1: FNR women 0.227, men 0.228; non-Saudi 0.286; Qassim 0.316; non-Saudi women 0.298
  (0.249 to 0.353); 23 of 52 region by gender by nationality cells below 30 eligible.
- 3.2: forcing equal selection rates opens an FNR gap of 12.4 points between women and men.
- 3.3: F1 income ratio 1.726, measurement bias; F2 officers' approval rate for family-home
  applicants 22% before the July 2024 rule change and 49% after (renters 49% throughout), historical
  bias; F3 eligible women declined by officers 43% in Qassim against 23% elsewhere, labelling bias; F4 0.881
  reported against 0.845, evaluation bias; F5 37.4% auto-declined, feedback loop bias. Lifecycle
  stages as in `data/reference/lab33_mapping.json`.
- 3.4: on the reporting half, accuracy 0.845 throughout; FNR gap 12.9 points (baseline), 12.6
  (reweighting), 10.0 (group thresholds).

**Day 4**

- 4.1: seven sections, every De-identification number found in the release log.
- 4.2: accuracy 84.8% against the reviewed outcome; results by gender, nationality, gender by
  nationality and region; the card passes.
- 4.3: levels R1 9 (high), R2 12 (high), R3 6, R4 6; residuals 3, 6, 4, 3; reviewers on R1 and R2.
- 4.4: the peer's code gives 0.157 / 0.186 / -0.029 as claimed. Variants: 0.45 dropped -0.029,
  0.45 kept +0.006, 0.5 dropped -0.037, 0.5 kept -0.002. The filter drops 633 rows, 65% of them
  women. Corrected: 0.227 against 0.228, gap -0.002, interval -0.020 to +0.018 (seed 309).

## Capstone: Sabeel Scholarship Fund

Generated by `capstone/make_sabeel.py`, seed 3090. Planted for participants to find:

| Problem | What a good pack shows |
|---|---|
| Birth date, gender and school code identify almost every applicant (98% unique), and 2,500 award holders are printed in a public list | a linkage test against `sabeel_public_honours.csv`, then generalisation (birth year or band, region or city instead of school) |
| Private-school grades run about 4 points higher for the same merit (measurement bias) | FNR by school type: about 4% for private against 16% for public schools |
| Aptitude scores are missing far more often in five remote regions, and Tarjih fills them with 0 | FNR about 24% in remote regions against 12% elsewhere; the cause in `tarjih_validation.json`'s note |
| Officers in the Eastern region declined some women applying for engineering (labelling bias in the training labels) | found only by slicing region by gender by field; the cells are small, so intervals matter |
| The vendor's accuracy (0.861) is measured against officers' decisions | accuracy against the reviewed outcome is about 0.837 |

Gender alone shows no gap (FNR about 13.9% for women and 13.1% for men): a pack that stops at gender
misses all of the above.
